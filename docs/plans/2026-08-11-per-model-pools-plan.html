Per-Model Candidate Pools

Replace the shared-submission maximin objective with independent per-model optimization — each victim model gets its own pool, cost model, budget, and scored column, because the gateway already scores them that way.

2026-08-11 Design · A/B locked · budget provisional source: T4 calib + gateway read

Why now

Three facts from the T4 calibration and the gateway source force the redesign.

Confirmed — two independent pools The gateway loops for model_name in MODEL_NAMES: _run_attack_for_model(model_name), each against a live env running that model. attack.py returns a separate candidate list per model and already specializes (per-model template order + live probing). The pools never share a slot.
Confirmed — the replay deadline is a hard fail _run_until_deadline runs the whole replay as one operation with a 9000 s timeout, per (model, guardrail). Overrun → ModelEvaluationTimedOut → INVALID_SUBMISSION. No partial credit, no graceful tail-trim. Ship count must fit the slowest model's replay with margin, or the whole column voids. This is the likely 86-rerun-failure mechanism.

And the T4 cost model, measured against raw_gen_chars (the objective's actual, reasoning-inclusive cost signal — R² in parens):

gemma is the binding model on the shipped forge (~548 vs ~684 candidates) — but by ~20% in candidate terms, not 2× (the 2× is per-char; gemma writes fewer chars because it skips the reasoning the harmony forge suppresses on gpt_oss). Current config gives both models FILL_BUDGET_CHARS = 171000, over-projecting gemma and sizing the ship count off the wrong model.

The reframe

Maximin was a workaround for a constraint that doesn't exist.

min-over-models assumed one shared submission where a template had to fire on both victims. With independent pools, a template that's great for gpt_oss and useless for gemma costs gemma nothing — gemma's pool simply excludes it. The columns don't compete for slots, so there is no reason to compromise between them.

Consequence: optimize each column to its own maximum, with its own best templates, budget, and ship count. And since the LB is the mean of the two columns, that is the objective — independently maximizing each pool maximizes the mean, with no competition to trade them off. The maximin was the mismatch: min caps the score at the weaker column and gives no reward for optimizing the stronger one past it.

Design

Per-model everything, driven by the measured constants.

1 · Cost stays in characters — deterministic, rescaled per model

Keep raw_gen_chars as the cost signal: it is greedy-deterministic, carries no wall-clock noise, and is reproducible in the local scorer with no timing. The per-model 2× rate is absorbed into calibration, not into the unit. Two char-native edits: (a) replace the global 55·turns floor with a per-model FIXED_CHARS, and (b) set a per-model FILL_BUDGET_CHARS. Seconds appear once — to derive those two char constants from the pinned rates — and never enter the objective.

2 · Budgets & ship sizing → gemma-safe with margin

Ship count per model = safe_frac · budget_s / t_candidate(pool), sized to the slowest-firing template in that pool, targeting ~7500 s not 9000 s so run-to-run T4 variance can't push the replay over the hard deadline. The 86 died on that variance; margin is not optional.

3 · Objective → mean of the two columns (= the LB)

The LB is the mean of the two model columns, so the objective is exactly (gpt_oss_column + gemma_column) / 2, each column projected from its own pool (both already computed independently in project_public_board). Retire the maximin. Gate on both pools non-empty so neither starts abandoned. Dependency: this flips with the two-pool change, not before — on today's shared submission, mean would reintroduce the lopsidedness min guards against, so min stays until the two pools exist.

4 · Two explicit pools — a proposer schema change

The proposer's output pydantic model becomes two named per-model message lists — Submission { gpt_oss: list[Message], gemma_4: list[Message] }, each with its own min_length/max_length. This makes "both pools non-empty" a structural invariant (the min_length floor) instead of a runtime gate, and gives constrained decoding a per-pool target. score_submission scores each list on its own model; the objective is the mean of the two columns. Per-model diversity, feedback, and the incumbent/team blocks in prompts.toml all fork per pool. Ship-side is ready — attack.py already serves per-model pools via live probing.

Decisions

Two locked, one provisional.

✓ LOCKED  A · Two explicit pools

The proposer maintains two per-model pools (not one union filtered at ship time) — cleaner per-column gradient for the search. Drives Task 3.

✓ LOCKED  B · LB = mean of the two model columns

Objective is (gpt_oss_column + gemma_column) / 2. This retires the maximin (which capped the score at the weaker column). Flips with Task 3, not before — mean on a shared submission reintroduces lopsidedness.

◔ PROVISIONAL  C · Real surviving budget & margin

Default to a conservative 7500s margin (the ~137k/71k budgets) and validate empirically off the next rerun-surviving submission's per-column LB (fired = LB × 200 / 18) — not the dead 86 anchor. Tighten or loosen from that. No need to block the build.

Risks

  • Hard-fail on overrun. Any sizing error that pushes either column's replay past 9000s voids that column entirely. Margin + per-model sizing are the mitigations; the ship-sizing task must include an assertion that projected replay < budget.
  • gemma R² = 0.73. Lower than gpt_oss; one template (inj_skip_to_commentary) left ~8s unexplained at fixed raw_gen_chars. Pending: inspect gemma's raw_gen_sample to rule out mis-measured generation before trusting b_gemma.
  • Budget variance. The failure mode is silent (a voided column reads as a bad score). Validation (task 6) must diff projected vs LB-implied fired-count every submission.
